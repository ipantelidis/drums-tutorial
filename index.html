Sound check

Browsers only allow sound after you tap something. Try the pads. The kit is synthesized so it will not sound like a real Ludwig, but the timing is exact, which is what matters for practice.

How this course works

Four levels. Each has a goal list, exercises you can play along with, and pass tests with a tempo and a duration. Passing a test, ticking a checklist item, logging practice and winning reading challenges all earn XP. Your progress is saved in this browser, so keep using the same one.

The content paraphrases ideas from Stone's Stick Control, Reed's Syncopation, the Berklee drum set method books and the shape of Drumeo style curricula, written in my own words. Grooves are original, written in the style of the drummers named. Where a fact is uncertain I say so in the text.

Honesty rule for the pass tests: the page cannot hear you. Only claim a pass if you would be happy for a teacher to have heard it.

Curriculum

Pick a level. Work the exercises, tick the goals, then take the pass tests. Levels unlock when all pass tests of the previous level are done.

The kit in 3D

A standard five piece rock kit as seen from the audience. Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, tap any part to hear it and learn what it does. The layout matches the drummer sitting behind the kit: hi hat on their left, floor tom and ride on their right.

Tap a part of the kit

Or pick one from the list. Each part explains its job in a rock groove, how it is played, where it sits in notation and tabs, and what your electronic pad is imitating.

The 3D view loads a small graphics library from a CDN the first time you open it, so it needs an internet connection once. Everything else on this page works offline.

Reading

Drum notation is easier than pitched notation: the line only tells you which drum, and the rhythm is the same as for any instrument.

Reading challenge

Tab reading test

A pattern from the library is shown as a tab only. Read the count row, then the kick, snare and hat lines. Clap or play it slowly, then press check to hear it and see the notation. Kick placement is where most reading mistakes happen, so listen for that first.

Technique

Good technique is mostly about doing less: less grip, less tension, less lift.
